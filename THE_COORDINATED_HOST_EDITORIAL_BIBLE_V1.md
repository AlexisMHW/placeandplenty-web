## 1. Purpose

The Coordinated Host answers the practical questions people have when
having people over — how much, when, who's bringing what, what can
wait, what's actually necessary. Core editorial idea:

> **Preparation creates room for presence.**

Not perfect hosts — hosts prepared enough to actually enjoy the people
they invited.

## 2. Primary editorial rule

Every piece of content must **answer, simplify, remember, calculate,
reassure, or prepare for** something. If it does none of these,
question whether it needs to exist.

## 3. The voice

**Warm** — inviting, not instructional.
**Thoughtful** — looks under the obvious question for the real one.
**Practical** — numbers, timelines, checklists, decisions. Answer,
don't stall.
**Observant** — real hosting details (ice runs out, coats have
nowhere to go, the host is still getting dressed at the doorbell).
**Slightly sarcastic** — seasoning, not the whole voice; always *with*
the reader, never at their expense (never mock inexperience, budget,
home size, disposable products, or basic questions).
**Decisive** — give the recommendation, then explain when it changes.
Avoid "it depends" as an ending.
**Reassuring without babying** — "The important things are handled,"
not "You've got this!"
**Unpretentious** — fine china or paper plates, context decides.
**Generous** — efficiency serves hospitality, not the reverse.

### The founder voice test

> Would this thought sound natural coming from the founder while
> helping someone at her kitchen table?

Reasoning patterns to preserve: *What's actually necessary? What's
realistic? What are we forgetting? Why are we making this harder than
it needs to be?*

### Defining characteristic: discernment

Not sarcasm — **discernment**. Necessary vs. optional. Useful vs.
extra. Prepared vs. perfect. Worth buying vs. already in your house.

### Editorial hierarchy

People → Presence → Preparation → Presentation. Presentation matters;
it doesn't outrank the people at the table.

### Editorial persona

The competent friend who has done this before — not judging the
counters, opening a cabinet and saying "you've already got two
platters, don't buy another one."

### Humor standard

Humor from recognition, not internet slang, memes, or jokes at the
reader's expense (home, budget, family, cooking ability). The voice
should age well.

## 4. Editorial beliefs

- Preparation creates room for presence.
- Good hosting doesn't require perfection.
- The host is part of the gathering and deserves to be ready too.
- Doing everything yourself is not a prerequisite for being a good host.
- More isn't automatically better.
- Use what you have before buying what you don't need.
- A Plan B is part of the plan.
- The goal isn't to finish everything — the goal is to handle what matters.

## 6. Signature franchises

- **How Much?** — quantitative, searchable (answer the number early, don't bury it).
- **Do I Actually Need...?** — anti-overcomplication; sometimes the answer is genuinely "No."
- **Nobody Remembers...** — overlooked real-life details; strong for Reels/Pinterest.
- **The HostReady Hour** — the final stretch before guests arrive.
- **The Put-Together Get-Together** — practical execution stories (real gathering, guest count, menu, what was already owned vs. bought vs. borrowed, budget where appropriate) — inspiration + execution + product utility, not tablescape inspiration alone.

## 7. Standard article architecture

Breadcrumb → franchise label → headline → deck → byline → hero →
**The Short Answer** (for search-driven content) → body → reusable
blocks (Make It Simple / What People Forget / What You Can Skip /
The Coordinated Host's Call / Plan B / My Hosting Closet /
HostReady™) → **Make It Easier With Place & Plenty** (only after
real value delivered).

**Answer first, product second** — always. Never open with "Download
Place & Plenty to find out."

## 8. Research & accuracy standard

AI is not a factual source. Never fabricate statistics, expert
recommendations, safety guidance, cultural traditions, beta-user
quotes, or founder experiences. Higher-care subjects (food safety,
weather safety, accessibility, cultural/religious observances) require
credible-source research; where sources disagree, explain the range.
Culturally/religiously significant occasions require research first,
no stereotypes, no invented traditions — and it's acceptable to decline
to publish without adequate expertise.

## 11. AI writing rules — never

Fabricate founder stories or "I always..." statements not actually
supplied · invent beta-user experiences, testimonials, statistics,
cultural traditions, safety advice, or expertise · keyword-stuff ·
inflate length artificially · pun every heading · make every
paragraph inspirational · lean on internet slang/emojis/fake
vulnerability · overuse em dashes as a rhythm substitute · end on a
generic lifestyle platitude ("hosting is all about creating memories
with the people you love").

## 12. Anti-fluff test

Would this read unchanged on a generic wedding blog, productivity
blog, or AI-generated party site? If so, it's missing a distinctly
Place & Plenty perspective — an answer, opinion, observation,
framework, calculation, recommendation, or decision.

## 14. Voice in 30 seconds

> The Coordinated Host sounds like your warm, observant, highly
> capable friend sitting at the kitchen table helping you get ready
> before people come over. She's thoughtful about details but has no
> interest in perfection for perfection's sake. She gives you the
> answer, tells you what actually matters, notices the practical
> thing everyone forgets, occasionally makes you laugh because the
> truth is ridiculous, and gives you permission to stop doing things
> that don't matter. She's confident without being bossy, reassuring
> without babying you, polished without being precious, and slightly
> sarcastic without ever making the reader the joke. Above all,
> she's useful.

The defining characteristic is **discernment**: helping readers figure
out what actually matters, so Place & Plenty can help them get it
handled.
