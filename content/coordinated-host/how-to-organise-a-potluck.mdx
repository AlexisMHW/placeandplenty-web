A potluck fails in one of three ways, and all three are preventable
before anyone cooks anything.

## Failure one: "bring a side"

Ask ten people to bring a side and you will get four potato dishes, two
identical salads, and no vegetable anyone actually wants.

This is not because your friends lack imagination. It is because "a
side" is a decision you have handed to someone standing in a shop with
no information about what anyone else is bringing. Faced with that, most
people pick the safe thing. The safe thing is potatoes.

**Ask for slots instead:**

- Two vegetables, one of them green
- One starch that is not the potatoes
- Bread
- Two desserts
- Ice, wine, something non-alcoholic

A specific request is also easier to say yes to. "Bring whatever" is
work. "Can you bring a green vegetable for eight" is a task, and people
would rather have a task.

## Failure two: the oven

This is the real constraint and almost nobody does the maths.

You have one oven. If four people arrive with dishes that each "just
need twenty minutes at 350", you have eighty minutes of oven and a cold
dinner. Ovens also do not run at two temperatures, so the person who
needs 400 and the person who needs 325 cannot both be right.

Two rules fix it:

1. **Tell people whether their dish can arrive needing the oven.** Most
   things can be told "bring it ready to eat, or ready to be room
   temperature." Say it in the invitation, not on the day.
2. **Keep the oven-dependent things yourself.** You control your own
   timing. You do not control when someone leaves their house.

Whatever comes out of your oven last should be the thing that matters
most. Everything else can wait on the counter under foil, and will be
fine.

## Failure three: the group chat

By the day before, the menu exists in forty messages across two threads,
one of which has three people in it who are not coming.

Someone has said "I'll bring something sweet" and nobody knows whether
that is a dessert or a bag of sweets. Two people have quietly both
decided to bring the bread. You are reconstructing dinner from scroll
history.

The fix is not a better group chat. It is having the list somewhere
that is not a conversation — anywhere both of you can look at the same
thing and see what is still open.

## What the host should keep

Keep what travels badly and what sets the timing:

- Anything that has to come out hot
- Gravy, sauces, anything that separates or skins over
- The centrepiece, whatever it is

Give away everything that travels well and everything that is fine at
room temperature. That is more of the meal than you would think.

## And the thing nobody says

Somebody will forget. Somebody will bring the wrong thing. Somebody will
bring three times too much of one item.

None of that ruins an evening. Running out of ice, having no clean
surface, and being in the kitchen when everyone arrives — those ruin
evenings. The menu being slightly lopsided does not.

Plan so the failures are the survivable kind.
