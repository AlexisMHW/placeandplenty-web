My favorite kind of party favor is another helping of something you already enjoyed.

Instead of buying a small object for everyone to take home, I like to put that money toward extra food or dessert and containers that make it easy to carry. It is a continuation of the gathering: a little more of a good time, ready for later.

The important word is planned. Making extra on purpose is different from buying far too much and hoping someone rescues it at the end of the evening.

## Holiday take-home planning: count households first

Decide whether your send-off is per guest or per household. For 20 guests across eight households, two cookies per guest means 40 cookies; a four-cookie household box means 32. Add these to what you plan to serve, not instead of it.

Choose one manageable option:

- Cookies or brownies in a food-safe box or bag.
- A slice of cake in a container tall enough to protect it.
- A chilled meal portion in a leakproof container for guests who can transport it safely.
- Soup in a leakproof food container with bread packed separately.

Check ingredients and dietary needs before assigning portions. Let guests decline without explanation.

## Budget for the whole package

Use this worksheet:

- Number of portions or household boxes: ____
- Extra ingredients: ____
- Food-safe containers: ____
- Labels or simple finishing touches: ____
- Total extra cost: ____
- Cost per box: total divided by boxes = ____

For an $80 allowance, you might reserve $60 for extra ingredients and $20 for containers. Those are planning limits; check actual prices. If packaging consumes most of the budget, simplify it.

## Pack safely and make the handoff easy

Set aside intended portions before serving and store them appropriately. Keep cold food refrigerated until departure. Tell guests what is inside, when it was made and how it should be stored. Follow [FoodSafety.gov's storage and cooling guidance](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety): refrigerate perishable food within two hours, or one hour when temperatures exceed 90°F. Use shallow containers for cooling. Plan chilled transport for perishable food; when that is not practical, choose a different favor.

A note is enough: “A little more of a good time. Thanks for coming.”
