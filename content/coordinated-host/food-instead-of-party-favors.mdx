I have a hosting opinion that might get me politely removed from the party-favor aisle: I don’t really believe in party favors.

At least, I don’t believe in spending money on a little something simply because everyone is supposed to leave with a little something. I want to know it will get used. I want it to feel connected to the gathering. And I would like my money to have done an actual job.

So I send people home with food. Or dessert. Sometimes both.

I take the money I would have spent on favors, put it toward strategically making extra of what I am already serving, and buy nice containers to send it home in.

**The favor is another helping of a good time.**

## I plan the take-home food before I cook

This is a decision I make when I plan the menu. I choose what will travel well, decide how much extra to make, and include the packaging in the budget.

That distinction matters. A pot of food left after a gathering might feed two people or twelve. A planned take-home portion has already been counted.

It also means I don’t need to create a whole separate favor project. If I’m baking dessert anyway, I can make more of that dessert. I don’t need twelve new recipes, a second theme, and an evening assembling tiny bags. There are already people coming to my house. I have things to do.

## Put the favor budget somewhere delicious

Here is an example, not a price promise: if you were going to spend $4 each on favors for 24 guests, that is $96. You could set aside $24 for containers and use the remaining $72 for extra ingredients or dessert portions. Check your actual food and packaging costs before deciding what fits.

The calculation is simple:

**Extra food + containers + any labels or finishing touches = the favor budget.**

The containers count. The ribbon counts. Buying an elaborate box for every cookie can quietly recreate the expense you were trying to avoid.

I would start with one generous, manageable option. A lovely slice of cake does not need three companion desserts to justify going home with someone.

## Choose something worth taking home

The best edible party favor fits both the menu and the trip home. A few possibilities:

- **Cookies or brownies:** easy to portion into small food-safe boxes or bags.
- **Slices of cake or pie:** use a container that protects the slice and gives the topping room.
- **A meal for tomorrow:** a portion of the main dish and a side, packed appropriately and kept at a safe temperature.
- **Soup and bread:** leakproof containers for the soup, with the bread packed separately.

These are options, not a requirement to offer all four. Choose for the people you are hosting, including their dietary needs, and what you can realistically prepare and store.

Count the take-home portions separately from the portions for the gathering. For example, if 24 people will each receive two cookies, reserve 48 cookies for the take-home plan **in addition to** the cookies you intend to serve. If you are sending one box per household instead, count households. Those are very different shopping lists.

## The nice container is part of the gesture

Presentation is where this becomes an intentional send-off. A sturdy bakery box, a neatly packed food container, or a simple bag with a small label can make something familiar feel considered.

Choose packaging made for food and suited to what is going inside it. Check the lid. Check the size. Check that a slice of cake will still look like a slice of cake after the drive.

A note can be as simple as:

> A little more of a good time. Thanks for coming.

There is no need to turn the container into another thing someone has to keep forever—or return to you next week.

## Make it easy to say yes, and fine to say no

Let guests know there is something to take home. Offer a choice when it is practical. Someone may love dessert but be traveling the next morning; someone else may be delighted to have lunch handled.

I want the food to be wanted. Sending everyone away with a huge portion they cannot use would miss the point just as much as buying an unwanted favor.

Set aside the intended portions appropriately before the serving dishes make their way around the room. Keep any ingredient information available, and include storage instructions where needed.

For perishable take-home food, keep the cold portions refrigerated until departure and plan for a chilled trip home. [FoodSafety.gov recommends](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety) refrigerating perishables within two hours, or one hour above 90°F, and using shallow containers for prompt cooling. A pretty box does not replace proper food storage.

## Something to remember the gathering by

If you love choosing traditional favors, enjoy them. My own money goes toward the food, the experience, and a thoughtful way to send a little of it home.

A dessert from the gathering carries a connection to the time we just spent together. Tomorrow, there is another slice. Another cookie. A small reminder of being fed and welcomed.

That is the kind of favor I believe in.

**Come over. Have a good time. Take something delicious with you.**
