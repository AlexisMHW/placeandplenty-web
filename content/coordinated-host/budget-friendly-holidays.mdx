There is a particular kind of holiday math that happens in your head. Dinner, gifts, something for the teacher, something for the doorway, something for the party you forgot was this weekend. Individually, each thing sounds reasonable. Together, they start looking like a second household budget.

You can love the season and still want it to cost less.

Start with the part you actually want to remember. Is it your people around the table? Baking with the children? A slow morning with music and something good in the oven? Choose three things that make the season feel like yours. Give those things first claim on your money and energy.

## Make a holiday plan in 20 minutes

1. **Pick three keepers:** write down the meal, activity, or tradition your household most wants to keep. Ask everyone before assuming.
2. **Set one spending ceiling:** use money you can comfortably allocate; do not start with a shopping list and call the total a budget.
3. **Divide it:** food and drinks, gifts, décor, activities, packaging and travel. Hold back a small unassigned amount for surprises.
4. **Inventory first:** check pantry, freezer, serving pieces, batteries, gift bags, ribbon and decorations. Mark each item owned, borrowed or needed.
5. **Make one shared decision:** agree with guests on a potluck, gift limit or simpler gathering format before anyone spends.
6. **Stop one task:** pick something you can skip without losing one of your three keepers.

## A budget worksheet you can copy

- Total comfortable spending limit: ____
- Food and drinks: ____
- Gifts and materials: ____
- Décor and wrapping: ____
- Activities and travel: ____
- Unassigned reserve: ____
- Already bought or committed: ____
- Remaining to spend: total limit minus all commitments = ____

Keep the household holiday budget separate from each gathering's budget so the same dollars are not promised twice. Review planned and actual spending after each shopping trip.

## What you can skip

A new theme for every room, a separate favor project, matching dishes, and a handmade gift you dislike making are optional. Put one meaningful detail where people will notice it: a favorite dessert, a familiar playlist, or a welcoming place to sit.
