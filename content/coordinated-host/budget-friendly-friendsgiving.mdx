Friendsgiving can be especially meaningful in a year when travel, gifts, and everyday expenses compete for the same dollars. For friends staying close to home—or simply needing a little company—it offers a reason to gather without recreating a grand holiday production.

The shared part needs to include more than the dishes. One person can still end up buying the main, cleaning the house, coordinating twelve texts, lending every serving bowl, and washing up afterward. That is a potluck with an unpaid production department.

Begin with an honest invitation: “We’re keeping this relaxed and sharing the meal. Bring a dish if that works for you, or choose a small job. We’d love to have you either way.” Contribution is an invitation to participate, not the price of admission.

## A shared menu for eight: assign roles, then check portions

Use this as a planning example, not a fixed recipe or an equal-spending requirement:

- Host: one main dish, enough for the eight confirmed guests, and tap water.
- Friend one: one vegetable side; confirm that it serves eight.
- Friend two: one substantial side or salad; confirm dietary needs first.
- Friend three: bread and one dessert, or one of those if both stretch the budget.
- Friend four: setup help, a playlist, or cleanup instead of buying food.

One household can take more than one role, but nobody needs to spend the same amount. Ask privately what is comfortable. Store-bought food counts.

## The contribution list needs six details

For every dish record **name, person bringing it, servings, relevant ingredients/allergens, arrival time, and reheating or serving needs**. Ask the contributor to bring a serving utensil and identify their container. A “side dish” entry cannot tell you whether dinner needs another vegetable or another oven.

## A timeline that avoids last-minute chasing

1. **Two weeks out:** choose a date, invite people, ask about dietary needs and offer food or job contributions.
2. **One week out:** confirm the headcount, fill menu gaps and agree on who brings drinks, ice and containers.
3. **Two days out:** check arrival times and oven needs. Put a name beside setup and cleanup jobs.
4. **On the day:** clear a landing space for dishes, show contributors where food belongs and keep the welcome easy.

## An invitation you can copy

> Come over for Friendsgiving! We’re keeping it relaxed and sharing the meal. Bring a dish if that works for you, or choose a setup or cleanup job. If this is a season when you simply need a seat, come anyway. Please tell me any dietary needs when you reply.
