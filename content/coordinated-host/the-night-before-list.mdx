Almost nothing on this list is cooking. That is the point.

The night before a gathering, the instinct is to get ahead on food. But
food is the one part that mostly cannot be rushed forward, and the
things that actually wreck a morning are never the food.

## 1. Clear the sink and one full counter

This is the highest-return thing on the list and it takes eleven
minutes.

Tomorrow you will need somewhere to put dirty things and somewhere to
put clean things. If the sink starts full, every task afterwards is
slower, and you will spend the last hour before guests arrive doing
washing-up instead of getting ready.

An empty sink and one clear stretch of counter is worth more than any
amount of prep-cooking.

## 2. Set the table

Not because it needs to be done early. Because doing it now tells you
what you are missing while shops are still open and while you still
care.

You will discover you have eleven forks, or that the serving bowl you
pictured is in the loft, or that the candles are stubs. All of that is
solvable tonight and unsolvable tomorrow at four.

## 3. Chill the drinks

Cold drinks are the difference between arriving and being welcomed, and
a fridge takes hours to do what ice cannot do quickly.

Getting drinks cold overnight also roughly halves the ice you need,
because the ice is then keeping things cold rather than making them
cold.

## 4. Pull out every serving dish you plan to use

Physically take them out and put them on the counter.

Two things happen. You find out you do not own a dish big enough for one
of the things you are making, while there is still time to change the
plan. And tomorrow, when everything is coming out of the oven at once,
you are not opening cupboards with oven gloves on.

If you know what is going in what, put a sticky note in each one. It
looks excessive and it saves ten minutes at exactly the moment you have
none.

## 5. Decide the first thirty minutes

Where do coats go. What is the first drink. What is already out to eat.

Guests arrive in a clump, and the first half hour sets whether the
evening feels relaxed or slightly frantic. If there is something on the
table and a drink in hand within four minutes of the door, everything
after that is easier.

You do not need a plan for the whole evening. You need a plan for the
first thirty minutes.

## 6. Put the bins out

Tomorrow will produce more rubbish than a normal day and you will not
want to deal with it mid-evening. Start empty.

## What not to do the night before

**Do not deep clean.** It will not survive the day and you will be tired
for the part that matters. Clean the bathroom, clear the surfaces,
hoover where people will actually be. Stop.

**Do not start something new and ambitious.** Eleven o'clock is when
people decide to also make a dessert. Eleven o'clock is not when that
decision is at its best.

**Do not rearrange furniture yet** unless you are certain. Do it in
daylight when you can see how people will actually move through the
room.

## The honest version

If you only do two of these, clear the sink and set the table.

Everything else is comfort. Those two are the ones that change what
tomorrow feels like.
