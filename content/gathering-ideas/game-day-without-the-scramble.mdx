Nobody loses a watch party on the food. They lose it on the clock.

The wings come out of the oven at kickoff. The dip is still cold in the
middle. Someone is opening the chips while the anthem plays, and you
watch the first drive from the kitchen.

The fix is not more food. It is deciding, in advance, what has to be hot
and what does not.

## Work backward from kickoff

Pick the time the game starts. Everything else is measured from there.

- **The day before.** Anything that improves overnight — chili, baked
  dips before they are baked, anything pickled, anything marinating.
- **Two hours out.** Cold things get plated and go back in the fridge on
  the tray you will actually serve them on.
- **One hour out.** The oven does one job at a time. Whatever needs it
  last, goes in last.
- **Twenty minutes out.** Ice. Drinks into the cooler. This is the step
  everyone forgets and it is the one guests notice.
- **Kickoff.** You sit down.

## Three things hot, everything else room temperature

A spread where everything is hot is a spread that needs you in the
kitchen all afternoon. Pick three things worth keeping warm — a dip, a
main, one side — and let the rest be good at room temperature.

Room temperature is not a compromise. A cheese board, a bowl of good
olives, cold shrimp, a slaw, chips with three things to put on them:
that is most of a table, and none of it needs you.

## How much, actually

For a three-hour game with a mixed crowd, per person:

- 6 to 8 pieces of "handheld" food across the whole spread
- 1 to 1.5 cups of anything dip-adjacent per four people
- 2 drinks in the first hour, then roughly one an hour after
- **1 pound of ice per person**, and then buy more than that

Ice is the single most commonly under-bought item at a watch party. It
is also the cheapest thing on the list.

## The halftime reset

Set one alarm for halftime. Not to cook — to walk the room. Refill what
is empty, take away what is done, put out one thing you held back. It
takes four minutes and it is the difference between a spread that dies
in the third quarter and one that lasts.

## What you already own

Before you buy another sheet pan or another slow cooker, check what is
already in the closet. Most people who host a game day twice a year own
more serving equipment than they think and buy a fourth trivet anyway.
